Lecture 5: Video AI and Video Script Generation

Unit 5: AI Productivity Tools and Multimodal Prompting

Topics: AI video tools, text-to-video generation, storyboarding, scene planning, video scripts, and educational or promotional videos.

By Dr. Nitin Mishra

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students should be able to:
  • Explain common AI capabilities used in video production.
  • Differentiate scriptwriting, storyboarding, text-to-video, and video editing.
  • Develop a concise script for a short educational or promotional video.
  • Write a prompt specifying scene, action, camera, style, duration, and audio.
  • Review generated video for continuity, factual accuracy, rights, and responsible use.

1. Introduction to Video AI

Video AI refers to AI-supported tools that help create, transform, analyse, or edit video content. Depending on the tool, a user may generate clips from text or images, create a storyboard, draft narration, add captions, remove backgrounds, translate speech, or assemble scenes.

Video generation is often iterative. A single prompt may not produce the exact action, character, camera movement, or timing intended. Planning scenes and reviewing short outputs can make the process more predictable.

3. A Simple Video Production Workflow

  1. Set the objective: Decide what the viewer should know, feel, or do after watching.
  2. Define the audience: Select language, detail level, tone, and length.
  3. Write the script: Draft the opening, key message, supporting scenes, and closing.
  4. Build a storyboard: Describe each scene, visual, action, narration, and approximate duration.
  5. Generate or capture visuals: Use text-to-video, image-to-video, recorded footage, or a combination.
  6. Edit: Arrange clips, refine timing, add narration, captions, and suitable audio.
  7. Review and publish: Check factual accuracy, continuity, accessibility, permissions, and final format.

4. Writing an Effective Video Script

A script is the plan for what viewers see and hear. A short video usually benefits from a clear opening, a small number of key points, and a memorable conclusion.

Script sectionFunctionExample
HookGain attention quickly“Can AI help you turn an idea into a useful project faster?”
ContextExplain the topic and relevanceIntroduce AI productivity tools for students
Main contentDeliver key information or demonstrate stepsShow writing, presentation, and image-generation examples
TakeawayReinforce the central messageAI helps with first drafts, but results need checking
Call to actionSuggest the next step, if appropriate“Try one tool and evaluate its output critically.”

Estimating narration length

As a rough planning estimate, clear narration often falls around 120–150 words per minute, depending on the speaker and subject. A 60-second video might therefore use about 120–150 spoken words, leaving enough time for pauses and visuals. This is a guideline, not a fixed rule.

5. Storyboarding and Scene Planning

A storyboard breaks a video into individual shots or scenes. It helps identify missing transitions and keeps visuals aligned with the narration.

SceneVisual/actionAudio or narrationApprox. duration
1. OpeningStudent looks at a blank project page, then opens a laptop“Every useful project begins with an idea.”0–8 seconds
2. ToolsQuick views of drafting, slide planning, and visual design“AI tools can help organise ideas and prepare first drafts.”8–25 seconds
3. Human reviewStudent checks sources and edits the draft“But accuracy, privacy, and judgement remain your responsibility.”25–45 seconds
4. ClosingStudent presents a finished project to classmates“Use AI as an assistant—and think critically.”45–60 seconds

Subject: [person, object, or topic].
Action: [specific visible action from beginning to end].
Setting: [location and background details].
Camera: [shot size, angle, and movement].

7. Practical Example: A Short Educational Video

Goal: Create a short explainer introducing responsible use of AI tools to engineering students.

Plan a 30-second educational video for undergraduate engineering students.
Use three scenes:
1. A student planning an assignment on a laptop.
2. The student uses AI to draft an outline and organise ideas.
3. The student checks the facts, edits the draft, and prepares the final work.
Visual style: realistic, positive, professional campus setting.
Use medium shots and gentle camera movement. Keep the student's clothing,
laptop, and location consistent across scenes.
Narration theme: AI can support productivity, but the student must verify
facts and contribute their own judgement.
Avoid legible fake interface text, invented statistics, visible logos,
and exaggerated claims.

For tools that generate only short clips, create each scene separately and assemble the clips in an editor. Check continuity of characters, lighting, locations, and objects.

8. Editing, Sound, and Accessibility

  • Pacing: Keep each scene long enough for viewers to understand the message.
  • Transitions: Use simple, consistent transitions that do not distract from the content.
  • Audio balance: Keep narration understandable and background music lower than speech.
  • Captions: Review automatically generated captions for spelling, names, timing, and technical vocabulary.
  • Visual consistency: Check character appearance, props, colours, and spatial continuity across shots.
  • Accessibility: Include accurate captions, readable on-screen text, and verbal descriptions of essential visual information where appropriate.
  • Format: Choose aspect ratio, resolution, and file format based on the intended platform.

9. Limitations and Responsible Use

  • Continuity errors: Objects, faces, clothing, or backgrounds may change between frames or clips.
  • Physical inaccuracies: Motion, reflections, object interactions, and cause-and-effect may look unrealistic.
  • Audio errors: Generated speech or captions may mispronounce or misrepresent technical terms.
  • Privacy and consent: Obtain permission before using identifiable people’s images, voices, or recordings.
  • Deepfakes and deception: Do not create misleading footage that falsely depicts a real person or event.
  • Copyright: Check rights for footage, music, images, fonts, and generated outputs under the tool’s terms.
  • Disclosure: Label AI-generated or altered content when required or when viewers could otherwise be misled.
  • Fact-checking: Verify claims in educational and promotional videos before publication.

10. Student Practical Activity

Task: Plan a 30–60 second educational or promotional video.

  1. Select a topic, audience, and one clear message.
  2. Write a script with an opening, two or three key points, and a closing.
  3. Create a storyboard with at least four scenes.
  4. Write a text-to-video prompt for each scene.
  5. Generate or mock up the scenes and assemble them in a video editor if available.
  6. Review continuity, captions, factual accuracy, audio, and permissions.
  7. Submit the script, storyboard, prompts, and a short reflection on revisions.

11. Review Questions

  1. What is Video AI? List four capabilities of AI video tools.
  2. Explain the stages of a simple video production workflow.
  3. What is a storyboard, and how does it help production?
  4. List at least six components of an effective text-to-video prompt.
  5. Why is scene-by-scene generation sometimes useful?
  6. How can captions and audio improve accessibility?
  7. Identify four limitations or risks of AI-generated video.
  8. What consent, copyright, and disclosure considerations should be checked before publishing?

Conclusion

Video AI can help with scriptwriting, storyboarding, scene generation, editing, and captions. Good results depend on careful planning, specific prompts, consistent scenes, accurate narration, and responsible review. AI may speed up production, but the creator remains responsible for the final video and its impact.
