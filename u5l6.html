Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students should be able to:
  • Differentiate speech-to-text and text-to-speech systems.
  • Explain how AI supports transcription, captions, voice generation, and audio summarisation.
  • Prepare suitable prompts for transcribing and structuring audio content.
  • Evaluate transcription quality and correct errors using the original recording.
  • Apply privacy, consent, accessibility, and responsible-use practices for audio AI.

1. Introduction to Speech AI

Speech AI refers to AI methods and tools that process spoken language or generate speech. These tools can convert spoken words into text, produce spoken audio from written text, create captions, summarise recordings, and help users interact with digital systems by voice.

Speech AI is useful in education, meetings, interviews, customer support, accessibility, language learning, and content production. Its output may still contain errors, especially with background noise, accents, overlapping speakers, specialised vocabulary, or low-quality recordings.

3. Speech-to-Text Workflow

  1. Capture audio: Record or upload audio that you are authorised to use.
  2. Prepare the recording: Reduce avoidable noise and ensure speech is audible.
  3. Select language and settings: Choose the language or supported transcription options when available.
  4. Generate transcript: Use an STT tool to create a first draft.
  5. Review against audio: Correct names, numbers, technical terms, speaker labels, and punctuation.
  6. Format the output: Add paragraphs, timestamps, speaker labels, or action items as needed.
  7. Protect the recording: Store and share the transcript according to consent and data policies.

4. Types of Transcription

TypeDescriptionBest suited for
VerbatimPreserves spoken words, including repetitions and filler words when requiredResearch interviews or legal contexts where exact wording matters
Clean verbatimRemoves some filler or repeated words while preserving meaningMeeting notes and general records
Edited transcriptReorganises or polishes speech for readabilityArticles or learning resources based on a recording
Timestamped transcriptIncludes time markers at intervals or key eventsVideo editing and searchable recordings
Speaker-labelled transcriptSeparates contributions by speaker where possibleInterviews, panels, and meetings
Important: Choose the transcription style before processing. An edited transcript is not a verbatim record and should not be presented as one.

5. Prompting for Accurate Transcription

If a tool accepts audio and text instructions, a clear prompt can specify the language, transcript style, formatting, and uncertainty handling. A prompt cannot guarantee perfect recognition; the result must still be checked.

Transcribe the supplied audio in [language].
Use [verbatim / clean verbatim] style.
Label speakers as Speaker 1, Speaker 2, and so on, only when distinguishable.
Add timestamps every [interval] if supported.
Preserve names, numbers, technical terms, and the original meaning.
If a word is unclear, mark it as [unclear] with a timestamp if possible;
do not guess.
After transcription, provide a separate list of uncertain words or phrases.
Do not summarise or add information that is not audible.

6. From Transcript to Meeting Notes

Transcription captures what was said. Meeting notes organise the important content into a more useful structure. These are different tasks and should be kept separate.

Example prompt

Using the verified transcript below, prepare meeting notes with these headings:
1. Main topics discussed
2. Decisions explicitly confirmed
3. Action items
4. Responsible person
5. Deadline
6. Open questions
Do not invent decisions, owners, or deadlines. If information is absent,
write “Not specified”. Distinguish proposals from confirmed decisions.
[Paste verified transcript here]

Review the notes against the transcript, particularly action items and decisions. A fluent summary can still omit important qualifications or misattribute a statement.

7. Text-to-Speech and Voice Generation

Text-to-speech systems convert written content into spoken audio. Depending on the tool, users may be able to select language, voice, speed, pronunciation, pauses, or emotional tone.

  • Prepare the text: Use clear sentences and expand abbreviations that may be pronounced incorrectly.
  • Choose language and voice: Select an appropriate voice for the audience and purpose.
  • Control pacing: Use punctuation and supported controls to improve natural pauses.
  • Check pronunciation: Listen for names, acronyms, technical terms, and number reading.
  • Review the result: Ensure tone, speed, and wording suit the intended context.

Example TTS prompt

Generate clear narration from the text below for an educational video.
Language: English.
Tone: calm, professional, and friendly.
Pace: moderate, with short pauses between sections.
Pronounce technical terms carefully. Do not add, remove, or paraphrase
the content. If pronunciation controls are available, flag words that
may need manual correction.

8. Audio Summarisation and Subtitles

Once a transcript has been checked, AI can help create concise summaries, chapter headings, revision notes, or subtitle drafts. The summary should not replace the original recording when exact wording matters.

  • For subtitles, check timing, line length, spelling, and synchronisation.
  • For summaries, preserve key claims, decisions, caveats, and unresolved questions.
  • For lecture notes, organise content by topic and define technical terms.
  • For multilingual content, ask a fluent speaker to review translations when accuracy is important.

10. Privacy, Consent, and Responsible Use

  • Obtain appropriate permission before recording or transcribing people, especially in private or sensitive settings.
  • Follow applicable institutional rules and laws concerning recording, consent, and retention.
  • Do not upload confidential meetings, personal records, or sensitive voice samples to unapproved services.
  • Do not clone or imitate a real person’s voice without appropriate authorisation and safeguards.
  • Clearly disclose synthetic or altered audio when people could reasonably be misled.
  • Keep access to recordings and transcripts limited to people who need them.
  • Check transcription accuracy before using it for research, assessment, official minutes, or consequential decisions.

11. Student Practical Activity

Task: Convert a short, non-sensitive audio recording into a checked transcript and structured notes.

  1. Use a short recording that you created yourself or have permission to use.
  2. Generate a first transcript using an available speech-to-text tool.
  3. Listen to the recording while checking names, numbers, and technical terms.
  4. Mark uncertain sections instead of guessing.
  5. Ask AI to convert the verified transcript into a summary or action-item table.
  6. Compare the summary with the transcript and correct omissions or invented details.
  7. Submit the original prompt, transcript, corrected version, and a brief quality-check report.

Conclusion

Speech AI supports transcription, narration, captions, and audio-based productivity. Reliable use depends on clear instructions, careful review against the original recording, and appropriate protection of voices and personal information. AI can assist with language processing, but people remain responsible for the accuracy and ethical use of the final output.
