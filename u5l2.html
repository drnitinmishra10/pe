By Dr. Nitin Mishra

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students should be able to:
  • Explain AI image generation and text-to-image systems.
  • Identify the main components of an effective image prompt.
  • Create prompts for posters, logos, and other graphic-design outputs.
  • Refine generated images by changing specific prompt elements.
  • Evaluate generated visuals for quality, relevance, accessibility, and responsible use.

1. Introduction to AI Image Generation

AI image generation is the use of artificial intelligence to create or modify visual content from instructions, reference images, or other inputs. A text-to-image tool interprets a written description and generates an image intended to match it. Some tools also support image editing, background replacement, style transfer, inpainting (editing a selected region), and outpainting (extending an image beyond its original boundaries).

These systems are useful for rapid concept development. They can help students explore visual ideas before producing a final design. However, a generated image may contain visual errors, inconsistent details, unreadable text, or elements that do not follow the brief. Human review remains essential.

3. How Text-to-Image Generation Works

  1. Prompt input: The user describes the subject, setting, style, and desired composition.
  2. Model interpretation: The AI maps the prompt to visual patterns learned during training.
  3. Image generation: The system creates one or more candidate images.
  4. Review and refinement: The user evaluates the result and adjusts the prompt or edits the image.
  5. Final use: The selected image is checked for quality, permissions, and suitability for its intended purpose.
Remember: A model generates an interpretation of the prompt, not a guarantee of exact compliance. Complex layouts, precise text, hands, small objects, diagrams, and technical details may need correction.

Reusable prompt formula

Main subject: [subject and key details].
Composition: [layout, framing, focal point, and empty space].
Visual style: [style or reference characteristics].
Colour and lighting: [palette and mood].
Format: [aspect ratio or dimensions].
Must include: [required elements].
Avoid: [unwanted elements, clutter, errors].

5. Practical Example: Educational Poster

Goal: Develop a concept poster for a student workshop on AI productivity tools.

Create a professional educational poster concept for a university workshop
titled “AI Productivity Tools for Engineering Students”.
Audience: undergraduate engineering students.
Visuals: a student working with a laptop, with simple visual symbols
representing writing, image creation, presentations, audio, and video.
Layout: portrait poster, strong visual hierarchy, clear title area at the
top, open space at the bottom for date, venue, and registration details.
Style: clean, modern, academic, minimal, high contrast.
Avoid: clutter, tiny text, fake institutional logos, watermarks, and
unnecessary decorative elements.
Generate the visual design; keep editable event details as placeholders.

Why it works: It identifies the purpose, audience, subject, layout, style, and restrictions. Keeping exact event details separate makes it easier to correct the final poster.

6. Practical Example: Logo Concept

Generate three distinct logo concepts for a fictional student innovation
club called “FutureLab”.
Purpose: represent creativity, technology, and collaboration.
Design: simple geometric symbol with a clear silhouette; readable at small
sizes; balanced icon and wordmark.
Style: professional, minimal, flat vector-like appearance.
Versions: black-and-white first, with no gradients.
Avoid: copied brand marks, complex detail, mockup backgrounds, and
unrequested slogans.
Present each concept separately with a short explanation of its idea.

AI-generated logo concepts should be treated as starting points. Before adopting a logo, check similarity to existing marks, refine the artwork in a design tool, and verify relevant ownership and trademark requirements.

7. Prompt Refinement: From Broad to Specific

Initial promptProblemRefined instruction
“Make a poster about AI.”Purpose, audience, format, and layout are unclear.Specify the event, student audience, portrait layout, title space, and visual style.
“Create a modern logo.”No brand meaning or practical constraints.Describe the organisation, values, symbol, simplicity, and small-size readability.
“Make it better.”Feedback is too general.“Reduce background clutter, increase contrast, and leave more space around the headline.”

Refine one or two elements at a time. This makes it easier to understand which change improved the result.

8. Composition and Visual Design Principles

  • Hierarchy: Make the most important information easiest to notice first.
  • Contrast: Use differences in size, weight, brightness, or shape to improve clarity.
  • Alignment: Place elements in a consistent visual structure.
  • Balance: Distribute visual weight so the design feels stable.
  • Whitespace: Keep sufficient empty space to avoid crowding.
  • Consistency: Use a limited set of fonts, colours, and visual styles.
  • Legibility: Ensure important text remains readable at the intended display size.

9. Limitations and Responsible Use

  • Visual errors: Inspect anatomy, object geometry, repeated elements, shadows, and perspective.
  • Text rendering: AI may misspell words or generate distorted lettering. Add exact text afterward in a suitable editor when precision matters.
  • Bias and representation: Check whether people, cultures, professions, and communities are represented fairly and appropriately.
  • Privacy: Do not upload private photographs or confidential material without permission and an approved workflow.
  • Copyright and style: Respect licences and rights. Avoid copying existing logos or requesting deceptive imitations of protected work.
  • Disclosure: Follow institutional or client rules about disclosing AI-generated or AI-assisted content.
  • Accessibility: Use readable contrast, meaningful alt text where supported, and do not rely on colour alone to communicate essential information.

10. Student Practical Activity

Task: Create a concept poster for a campus event using an AI image-generation tool.

  1. Choose an event topic, such as a coding competition, AI workshop, or sustainability drive.
  2. Write a prompt using at least six prompt components from Section 4.
  3. Generate an initial image.
  4. Review the image for layout, readability, relevance, and errors.
  5. Revise the prompt at least twice, describing specific changes.
  6. Add exact event text in a design editor if the image tool does not render it accurately.
  7. Submit the final image, original prompt, revised prompts, and a short reflection on the changes.

11. Review Questions

  1. What is AI image generation? Explain text-to-image generation in simple terms.
  2. List six components of an effective image prompt.
  3. How would you prompt an AI tool to create a university event poster?
  4. Why should exact event text often be added or checked in a design editor?
  5. Explain hierarchy, contrast, and whitespace in graphic design.
  6. What is the difference between inpainting and outpainting?
  7. Identify three ethical or legal considerations when using AI-generated visuals.
  8. Why is it useful to refine a prompt in small, specific steps?

Conclusion

AI image-generation tools support rapid visual exploration for posters, logos, illustrations, and design concepts. Better results come from specific prompts, clear composition instructions, iterative refinement, and careful human review. AI can accelerate the design process, but the final visual should still meet quality, accessibility, originality, and permission requirements.
