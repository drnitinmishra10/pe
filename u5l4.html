Lecture 4: Document AI and Spreadsheet AI

Unit 5: AI Productivity Tools and Multimodal Prompting

Topics: AI-assisted document creation, summarisation, formatting, spreadsheet analysis, formulas, data cleaning, tables, and charts.

By Dr. Nitin Mishra

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students should be able to:
  • Explain how AI supports document-related tasks and spreadsheet workflows.
  • Use prompts to draft, summarise, restructure, and proofread documents.
  • Identify spreadsheet AI tasks such as formula assistance, data cleaning, analysis, and chart selection.
  • Validate AI-generated formulas and data interpretations.
  • Apply privacy, accuracy, and responsible-use practices when working with documents and data.

1. Introduction

Document AI and Spreadsheet AI refer to AI-supported capabilities that help users create, understand, organise, and analyse information. Document tools focus on written material such as reports, letters, policies, meeting notes, and study resources. Spreadsheet tools assist with tabular data, formulas, summaries, patterns, and visualisations.

These tools can reduce repetitive work, but they do not remove the need to review outputs. A summary can omit an important condition, a formula can use the wrong cell range, and an apparent trend can be caused by missing or inconsistent data.

3. A Good Prompt for Document Tasks

Act as [role or perspective].
Task: [draft, summarise, rewrite, proofread, or extract].
Audience and purpose: [who will read it and why].
Source material: [paste approved text or describe the facts].
Requirements: [tone, length, sections, details to retain].
Output format: [email, report, bullet list, table, or minutes].
Accuracy rule: Do not invent facts. Flag missing information and preserve
important conditions, dates, names, and qualifications.

Example: Meeting minutes

Convert the notes below into concise meeting minutes.
Use these headings: Meeting Purpose, Key Discussion, Decisions,
Action Items, Responsible Person, and Deadline.
Keep decisions separate from suggestions. If an owner or deadline is
not supplied, write “Not specified” rather than guessing.
Source notes:
[Paste meeting notes here]

This prompt helps distinguish confirmed decisions from ideas and avoids invented responsibilities or dates.

5. Spreadsheet Fundamentals

  • Workbook: A spreadsheet file that can contain multiple worksheets.
  • Worksheet: A grid of rows and columns.
  • Cell: A single location identified by a column letter and row number, such as B4.
  • Range: A group of cells, such as B2:B10.
  • Formula: An expression used to calculate a result, usually beginning with an equals sign.
  • Function: A predefined operation such as SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, IF, or COUNTIF.
  • Data type: The kind of value stored, such as text, number, date, or logical value.

6. Example Spreadsheet Formulas

Suppose cells B2 through B6 contain marks: 78, 84, 69, 91, and 88. These examples illustrate common formulas; confirm the cell range and spreadsheet application before use.

PurposeExample formulaMeaning
Total=SUM(B2:B6)Adds values in the range
Average=AVERAGE(B2:B6)Calculates the arithmetic mean
Highest mark=MAX(B2:B6)Returns the largest value
Lowest mark=MIN(B2:B6)Returns the smallest value
Count numeric values=COUNT(B2:B6)Counts cells containing numbers
Conditional label=IF(B2>=40,"Pass","Review")Returns a label based on a condition
Formula safety: A formula may be syntactically valid but logically wrong. Check whether the pass threshold, range, treatment of blank cells, and rounding rules match the task.

7. Prompting AI to Help with a Formula

I have a spreadsheet with student marks in cells B2:B41.
Suggest a formula that labels a mark of 40 or above as “Pass” and
a mark below 40 as “Review”. Explain the formula and state where to enter it.
Assume blank cells should remain blank. Give an example for a blank cell
and a numeric mark. Do not modify any data.

An appropriate formula for this requirement in a spreadsheet that supports these functions is:

=IF(B2="","",IF(B2>=40,"Pass","Review"))

Enter the formula in the relevant result cell, then fill it down as appropriate. Verify the result on blank, boundary, and ordinary values before applying it to the full dataset.

8. Data Cleaning and Quality Checks

Data cleaning means identifying and correcting problems that can affect analysis. AI may suggest checks, but changes should be based on known rules and the original data.

  • Missing values: Identify blank cells and decide whether to leave, exclude, or fill them using a justified method.
  • Duplicates: Check whether repeated rows are genuine duplicates or legitimate repeated events.
  • Inconsistent formats: Standardise dates, names, categories, and units when the intended format is known.
  • Outliers: Investigate unusual values rather than automatically deleting them.
  • Invalid values: Apply domain rules, such as permitted ranges, only when those rules are established.
  • Audit trail: Keep a copy of the original data and record material changes.

9. Spreadsheet Analysis and Charts

Before analysing data, clarify the question. Examples include: Which category has the highest count? How did values change over time? Is there a relationship between two numeric variables?

QuestionPotential methodImportant check
What is the total or average?SUM, AVERAGE, or a summary tableCorrect range and units
How do categories compare?Bar or column chartConsistent categories and scale
How does a value change over time?Line chartDates in correct chronological order
How are two numeric variables related?Scatter plotAssociation does not prove causation
How are records distributed across groups?Frequency table or suitable chartClear group definitions and denominators

Example analysis prompt

Review the column names and sample rows I provide.
First identify possible data-quality issues and ask questions where
the meaning is unclear. Then suggest suitable summary statistics and
charts for the stated research question.
Do not invent values or claim that a pattern exists unless it is supported
by the supplied data. Separate observations from possible explanations.

10. Document AI and Spreadsheet AI: Combined Workflow

  1. Use a spreadsheet to organise and validate the data.
  2. Calculate results with verified formulas or analytical tools.
  3. Ask AI to help explain the verified results in plain language.
  4. Draft a report with methods, findings, limitations, and recommendations.
  5. Cross-check every number and statement against the spreadsheet.
  6. Remove confidential data from the output unless sharing is authorised.

11. Privacy, Ethics, and Responsible Use

  • Do not upload identifiable student records, salary details, medical records, passwords, or confidential institutional data to unapproved AI services.
  • Use anonymised or synthetic examples for practice whenever possible.
  • Do not rely on AI alone for consequential decisions about people.
  • Check formulas, calculations, and summaries independently.
  • Preserve the original dataset and document transformations.
  • Follow institutional rules for disclosure, data retention, and approved tools.

12. Student Practical Activity

Task A: Document AI

  1. Prepare a short set of fictional meeting notes.
  2. Prompt an AI tool to convert them into structured minutes.
  3. Check that decisions, owners, and deadlines have not been invented.

Task B: Spreadsheet AI

  1. Create a small sample dataset with at least 10 rows using fictional data.
  2. Ask AI to suggest formulas for total, average, and a simple conditional label.
  3. Test formulas on normal, blank, and boundary values.
  4. Create one appropriate chart and write a three-sentence summary of the verified result.
  5. Submit the prompts, formulas, checks, and final summary.

13. Review Questions

  1. What is Document AI? Give four examples of its uses.
  2. How can AI assist with meeting minutes while avoiding invented information?
  3. Define workbook, worksheet, cell, range, formula, and function.
  4. Explain the purpose of SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, and IF.
  5. Why must an AI-suggested spreadsheet formula be tested?
  6. What are four common data-quality problems?
  7. Which chart types are useful for category comparisons, time trends, and relationships?
  8. List three privacy precautions when working with spreadsheets and AI.

Conclusion

Document AI can support drafting, summarisation, and editing, while Spreadsheet AI can assist with formulas, data cleaning, analysis, and reporting. Reliable use requires clear prompts, verified calculations, careful handling of data, and human review of every important result.
